Methodology, sources and limitations

Grid Watch is a living evidence map, not a statutory register. Britain does not publish one definitive public list covering every private, edge, enterprise, operational and proposed data centre. Coverage is built by combining independent public/open sources and showing provenance.

Verified register: manually checked against planning authorities, grid/network records, government publications, company primary sources and reputable reporting. Unknown fields stay unknown rather than being guessed.

Electricity layer: 132 kV+ substations are mapped from OpenStreetMap. Their presence nearby is geographic context only and does not establish the connection point for a data-centre project. National queue figures are sourced separately from Ofgem.

Open map layer: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Community-built coverage can be incomplete or wrong. Grid Watch refreshes this layer automatically and links back to the OSM object.

Planning: a filing shows an application exists; it does not prove construction or operation. Planning records should link to the originating public record wherever possible.

Power: connected/operational, contracted/accepted, proposed/requested and estimated figures are different categories. Grid Watch does not present requested capacity as current electricity consumption.

Locations: distances are approximate and postcodes are geocoded using postcodes.io. Methodology is documented in the public repository.
