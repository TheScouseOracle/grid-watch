👀 What am I looking at?

Grid Watch puts different kinds of infrastructure and evidence on one map. They do not all mean the same thing.
🖥️ DATA CENTRE
A building or site containing computer servers and digital infrastructure. Some are relatively small; others are huge campuses. A data-centre marker does not, by itself, tell you how much electricity the site uses.
⚡ ELECTRICITY SUBSTATION — this is NOT a data centre.
Think of a substation as a major junction in the electricity network. It helps switch, transform and move electricity around the grid. Grid Watch shows larger 132 kV+ substations because large data centres can require substantial grid capacity. A nearby substation does not prove it supplies a nearby data centre.
🏗️ PLANNING ACTIVITY
This means something relevant has appeared in an openly licensed planning dataset. It does not mean it has been built. It may be proposed, awaiting a decision, approved, refused or otherwise recorded in planning data.
🔗 OWNERSHIP EVIDENCE
Where the open data contains an explicit organisation identifier, Grid Watch can follow open ownership or parent-organisation links. I do not guess a company simply because a name looks similar.
🔎 So what am I actually looking for?
Don't look at one dot and jump to a conclusion. Look for the pattern. What becomes interesting is when evidence starts joining up: data-centre proposal → planning application → power requirement → grid infrastructure → ownership → construction → operation.
👁️ What can I keep an eye on?
Watch for new planning applications, extensions to existing sites, new substations or electricity infrastructure, large proposed MW figures, changes in ownership, construction starting on proposed sites, and projects moving from proposed → contracted → connected.
The Scouse Oracle rule: The map is the starting point of the investigation — not the conclusion.

Methodology, licences and limitations

Strict open-data policy: the v2 automated database ingests only data with an explicit open licence or public-domain dedication. It does not wholesale-copy commercial data-centre directories, paywalled databases or council registers whose reuse terms have not been verified.

OpenStreetMap: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Community mapping can be incomplete or wrong. Data-centre and 132 kV+ substation layers refresh automatically.

Ownership: Wikidata CC0 is used only where an OSM record contains an explicit Wikidata identifier. Parent-company links are open linked data, not a claim that Wikidata is legally authoritative.

Planning: England and Northern Ireland are currently collected from explicitly OGL datasets. Scotland's official dataset is also OGL, but its automated download is access-key gated unless configured. Wales remains a declared national planning-data gap under this strict policy. A filing is evidence of an application, not evidence of construction or operation.

Power: numbers found in open planning descriptions are shown as text mentions until their meaning is established. Connected, contracted, proposed and estimated values are not interchangeable.

Completeness: open-data-only does not mean a complete market register. No single statutory UK dataset lists every private, edge, enterprise, operating and proposed data centre. Grid Watch publishes coverage gaps rather than inventing certainty.

Locations: distances are approximate and postcodes are geocoded using postcodes.io. Full methodology and source licences are documented in the repository.
